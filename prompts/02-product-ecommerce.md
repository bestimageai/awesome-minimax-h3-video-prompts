# Product and E-commerce Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for truthful feature demonstrations, marketplace clips, material studies, and conversion-oriented product video.

## PRD-001 Modular Hiking Lantern Feature Demo

**Use it for:** Showing three real product functions without changing the product design.

**Mode:** Product images + optional hand-motion reference  
**Format:** 4:5, target 10 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = front, side, and open-state geometry; Video 1 = hand movement only.

```text
Create a clean field-use demonstration for the fictional “TRAIL ARC” modular lantern. Reconstruct one consistent product from Images 1–3: charcoal cylindrical body, amber light ring, folding steel handle, removable frosted diffuser. Do not merge viewpoints into a new design.

0–3s: on a dark wooden camp table at blue hour, an adult hand lifts the folded handle and the lantern turns on at low warm brightness; camera remains at a stable three-quarter close-up. 3–7s: the same hand twists and removes the frosted diffuser in one mechanically plausible motion, revealing the focused downward task light; cut only after the part clears the body. 7–10s: lantern hangs from a tent loop, illuminating a map and mug while the background stays dim; settle on the product profile.

Use real weight, hinge resistance, contact shadows, metal highlights, and warm light falloff. Sound intent: hinge click, soft twist, canvas movement, night insects. Avoid extra controls, impossible disassembly, glowing brand text, floating parts, hand fusion, changing materials, fire effects, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Product geometry across states, hand contact, hinge motion, truthful light behavior, and no invented functions.

## PRD-002 Ceramic Diffuser Material Film

**Use it for:** A sensory e-commerce video focused on surface, vapor, and quiet motion.

**Mode:** Product image + material macro image  
**Format:** 1:1, target 8 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = whole product and proportions; Image 2 = ceramic glaze texture only.

```text
Generate a square material film for a fictional ceramic aroma diffuser. Preserve the low oval form, off-white lid, narrow vapor opening, and small walnut control from Image 1. Apply only the fine speckled glaze texture from Image 2; do not copy its shape.

Start with an extreme macro of side light moving across the fired ceramic grain. Slowly pull focus to the walnut control as one fingertip presses it with natural resistance. A thin, continuous ribbon of vapor begins at the opening, bends gently in room air, and remains translucent. Camera eases into a top three-quarter view, revealing the full object on a pale limestone shelf beside one folded towel; end after the vapor stabilizes.

Palette: chalk, oat, walnut, soft gray. Sound intent: quiet button click, nearly silent room tone, no music required. Avoid thick smoke, colored vapor, liquid leaking through ceramic, glossy plastic appearance, new seams, label text, levitation, excessive props, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Ceramic texture, subtle vapor physics, control placement, product silhouette, and restrained styling.

## PRD-003 Marketplace Rotation Without Geometry Drift

**Use it for:** A concise marketplace listing clip that reveals all sides of a reusable bottle.

**Mode:** Multi-view product images  
**Format:** 1:1, target 6–8 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–4 = front, back, left, and right views of the same product.

```text
Create a neutral marketplace rotation of the exact reusable bottle shown in Images 1–4. Resolve the inputs as one object: matte sea-blue body, straight walls, shallow shoulder, silver threaded cap, small vertical measurement window, white “CURRENT” wordmark on the front only.

The bottle stands centered on a light gray seamless surface. Camera is locked. The turntable rotates clockwise at constant speed for one complete, smooth view; the bottle does not wobble or change scale. Maintain the same cap threads, measurement window, base thickness, wordmark size, and surface finish through the full rotation. Use soft paired studio lights so highlights travel consistently around the cylinder; include one subtle contact shadow and no reflection.

No hands, liquid splash, props, labels on the back, extra ports, changing cap height, mirrored text, background gradient pulses, speed ramps, or watermark. End with the front view aligned exactly as the opening frame for a clean loop.
```

**Review:** Cross-view reconstruction, one full rotation, text orientation, highlight continuity, and loop alignment.

## PRD-004 Two-Tray Lunchbox Packing

**Use it for:** A marketplace clip that demonstrates actual compartment geometry.

**Mode:** First-frame plus product-detail images  
**Format:** 16:9, 10 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = empty terracotta lunchbox, two cream trays and teal lid; Image 2, if supplied, = approved lid closure detail.

**Adjust:** Approved food portions; retain exactly two trays.

```text
Create a clean tabletop packing demonstration of the lunchbox in Image 1. The opening frame contains one outer box, two empty cream inner trays and one separate dark teal lid. Use a fixed three-quarter overhead camera.

0–3s: an adult hand places three apple wedges in the left tray, one at a time. 3–6s: place one small wrapped sandwich in the right tray without moving the divider. 6–9s: bring the existing lid from the tabletop onto the box using only the closure mechanism shown in the references. 9–10s: hold on the closed lunchbox.

Use soft window light, realistic hand contact, quiet food and lid sounds, no narration. Preserve each tray's proportions and never create extra compartments, duplicate food, merge fingers, invent latches or imply leakproofness, heating safety or nutritional benefits.
```

**Review:** Two trays throughout; no object duplication; mechanically plausible lid; claims limited to visible packing.

## PRD-005 Knitted Sleeve Fit Demonstration

**Use it for:** Showing a laptop sleeve fit without inventing dimensions or protection claims.

**Mode:** Multi-view product images  
**Format:** 1:1, 8 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = empty knitted sleeve; Image 2 = the approved closed laptop; Image 3 = true fit reference.

**Adjust:** Yarn color; use only a device that fits the approved reference.

```text
Show the exact charcoal knitted sleeve and unbranded closed laptop from the references on a pale desk. Do not infer dimensions from perspective. Camera stays in a close three-quarter view with the full opening visible.

0–2s: two adult hands gently open the sleeve mouth without stretching its width unnaturally. 2–6s: the closed laptop slides into the sleeve in one slow movement, aligned with the reference fit. 6–8s: the hands release it and smooth one natural fold; keep a small approved top edge visible.

Maintain knit scale, seam placement, thickness and device proportions. Sound is a soft fabric rub and subtle desk contact. No drop tests, waterproof claims, shock-absorption graphics, size labels, altered laptop logo or impossible elastic expansion. End with the sleeve resting on the desk.
```

**Review:** Consistent textile scale and device size; no fit gained by changing geometry.
