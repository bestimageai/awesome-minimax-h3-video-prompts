## ANI-001 Paper-Cut Wetland Food Web

**Use it for:** An educational animation with a clear ecological relationship and tactile craft style.

**Mode:** Species reference sheet + layout sketch + optional narration rhythm  
**Format:** 16:9, target 12 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = accurate silhouettes of five wetland species; Image 2 = layer order; Audio 1 = timing only, not words.

```text
Create a layered paper-cut animation explaining one simple wetland food chain. Use exactly five recognizable subjects from Image 1: algae, water snail, small fish, heron, and decomposer fungi. Use Image 2 for spatial order only. All elements are hand-cut colored paper with visible fibers, shallow physical shadows, and hinge-like motion—no photoreal animals.

Beat 1: blue paper water slides into frame; algae layers unfold. Beat 2: a water snail moves toward algae, followed by a small fish entering from the opposite side; thin cream paper arrows appear one at a time to show algae → snail → fish. Beat 3: a heron silhouette lowers its beak toward the fish; fallen brown paper leaves descend and fungi shapes appear beneath, completing the cycle with one final curved arrow.

No statistics or generated labels; reserve a clean lower strip for human-added captions. Use gentle page sounds and wooden percussion. Avoid extra species, reversed arrows, realistic predation, plastic 3D rendering, tiny text, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Species count, arrow direction, material consistency, educational accuracy, and caption-safe space.

## ANI-002 Clay Repair Robot Finds a Button

**Use it for:** A charming stop-motion character short with clear object scale and personality.

**Mode:** Character turntable + workshop image + movement reference  
**Format:** 4:3, target 12 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = exact clay robot design; Image 2 = miniature workshop; Video 1 = stop-motion cadence only.

```text
Create a handcrafted clay stop-motion scene. Preserve the small repair robot from Image 1: mustard-yellow clay body, square head, two black bead eyes, one blue fabric tool pouch, wire arms with red mitten hands. Use the miniature wooden workshop from Image 2 without adding machines.

The robot hears a tiny metallic ping, tilts its head in two stop-motion increments, and walks three uneven steps across the bench. It finds a pearl button beside a spool, tries to lift it with one hand, slips backward, then uses both hands and places it proudly into the blue pouch. End with the robot patting the pouch once as a loose thread springs upward behind it.

Keep visible fingerprints, small pose-to-pose motion, stable scale, and warm practical lamp light. Sound intent: clay foot taps, metal ping, fabric rustle, soft wooden knock. Avoid fluid CGI motion, changing limb count, button resizing, new tools, speaking mouth, glossy plastic surfaces, text, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Character model, stop-motion cadence, object scale, two-hand interaction, and tactile materials.

## ANI-003 Ink Fish Becomes a City Tram

**Use it for:** A graphic transition film combining brushwork, transport, and urban rhythm.

**Mode:** Ink style sheet + tram reference + optional rhythm audio  
**Format:** 16:9, target 10 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = ink density and brush edge only; Image 2 = fictional tram geometry; Audio 1 = transition rhythm only.

```text
Create a restrained black-ink and vermilion animation on warm rice paper. A single hand-painted fish swims from left to right, leaving an irregular dry-brush wake. Its body stretches into one continuous horizontal stroke; three square ink marks become windows; the tail folds into the rear of the exact compact tram shown in Image 2. The transformation must be continuous and readable, not a cut.

Once formed, the tram travels along two thin brush lines through a minimal city suggested by six vertical ink blocks. At each stop, one vermilion seal-like circle pulses with Audio 1; no readable characters appear. The tram reaches the right edge, dissolves into a final ink wash, and the wash curls back into the opening fish silhouette for a loop.

Maintain paper grain, controlled ink bleed, and two-color discipline. Avoid calligraphy text, borrowed seals, photoreal city elements, extra vehicles, 3D reflections, broken transformation geometry, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Transformation continuity, tram silhouette, color discipline, rhythmic pulses, and seamless loop.

## ANI-004 Paper Lighthouse Beacon

**Use it for:** A tactile animation loop with one simple light action.

**Mode:** First-frame image  
**Format:** 16:9, 10 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = paper lighthouse, ochre keeper, lantern and folded sea.

**Adjust:** Paper palette; keep the keeper and lighthouse proportions fixed.

```text
Animate the original paper lighthouse scene in Image 1 as handcrafted stop motion. Preserve all paper folds, fibers, cut edges and the keeper's ochre coat. The lighthouse is ivory, the rock charcoal and the folded sea deep blue.

0–3s: the tiny keeper raises the paper lantern once while remaining at the lighthouse base. 3–7s: a warm beam from the lighthouse window sweeps slowly across the folded sea; the paper sea moves only in small hinged ripples, not liquid simulation. 7–10s: the beam returns to its initial direction and the keeper lowers the lantern to the opening pose.

Keep the camera locked. Sound: delicate paper creaks, a soft wind bed and one quiet lantern click. No speech, flames, new boats, melting paper, extra characters or text. Aim for a loop but inspect the seam before claiming seamless output.
```

**Review:** Stable cut-paper identity; one beam; no fluid-like paper deformation; loop seam checked.

## ANI-005 Clay Parcel Sorting Mistake

**Use it for:** A wordless animated comedy with a countable correction.

**Mode:** Character and prop references  
**Format:** 1:1, 12 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = original teal clay mole; Image 2 = one round and one square parcel with matching sorting openings.

**Adjust:** Parcel colors; no written addresses or shipping logos.

```text
Create a tactile clay animation in a miniature sorting room. One friendly teal clay mole stands beside exactly two parcels, one round and one square, and two matching shaped openings. Preserve fingerprints and slightly uneven clay surfaces.

0–4s: the mole lifts the round parcel toward the square opening, pauses before contact and tilts its head. 4–8s: it sets the parcel back down, then gently rotates its body toward the round opening. 8–12s: the round parcel passes through the matching opening; the square parcel remains visible and untouched. End on a small relieved shrug.

Fixed medium-wide camera, soft toy-stage lighting, clay taps and a muted slide sound. No dialogue, printed labels, additional limbs, parcel duplication or squeezing a rigid parcel through the wrong opening.
```

**Review:** Two parcels tracked; joke understandable without words; no geometry cheating.
